Agile Methodology Terms
1. Agile
·       Agile is a software development approach that focuses on flexibility and continuous improvement.
·       Work is divided into smaller parts.
·       Teams deliver working software frequently.
·       Customer feedback is used to improve the product.
2. Scrum
·       Scrum is an Agile framework used to manage software development.
·       Work is completed in short periods called sprints.
·       The team holds regular meetings to discuss progress.
·       It defines roles, events, and work items.
3. Sprint
·       A sprint is a fixed period during which the team completes selected work.
·       It usually lasts one to four weeks.
·       The team works toward a specific sprint goal.
·       At the end, the team reviews the completed work.
4. Iteration
·       An iteration is a repeated development cycle.
·       The team plans, develops, and tests a small part of the product.
·       Feedback is collected after each cycle.
·       It helps improve the product step by step.
5. User Story
·       A user story describes a feature from the user's point of view.
·       It explains what the user needs and why.
·       It is usually written in simple language.
·       Example: “As a user, I want to reset my password so that I can access my account.”
6. Epic
·       An epic is a large requirement that can be divided into smaller user stories.
·       It represents a major feature or business requirement.
·       It may take several sprints to complete.
·       Example: An online shopping system can have an epic called “Order Management.”
7. Story Point
·       Story points estimate the effort required to complete a user story.
·       They consider complexity, effort, and uncertainty.
·       Teams may use values such as 1, 2, 3, 5, and 8.
·       They are relative estimates, not fixed hours.
8. Acceptance Criteria
·       Acceptance criteria define the conditions a feature must satisfy.
·       They help the team understand when a user story is acceptable.
·       They guide developers and testers during implementation and testing.
·       Example: A login page must display an error message when the password is incorrect.


Scrum Roles and Responsibilities
1. Product Owner (PO)
·       Defines and communicates the product goals.
·       Manages and prioritizes the Product Backlog.
·       Understands customer and business requirements.
·       Ensures the team works on valuable features.
2. Scrum Master (SM)
·       Helps the team understand and follow Scrum.
·       Removes or helps resolve obstacles affecting progress.
·       Facilitates Scrum events when needed.
·       Helps the team improve its working practices.
3. Developers
·       Design, develop, test, and integrate the product.
·       Plan how to complete the Sprint Backlog work.
·       Work together to achieve the Sprint Goal.
·       Maintain the quality of the product.
4. Stakeholders
·       Include customers, users, managers, and other interested people.
·       Share business needs and expectations.
·       Provide feedback about the product.
·       Help the Product Owner understand priorities.


Scrum Events and Their Purpose
1. Sprint Planning
·       Takes place at the beginning of a sprint.
·       The team decides what work can be completed.
·       The Product Owner explains important backlog items.
·       The team establishes a Sprint Goal and plans the work.
2. Daily Scrum
·       A short daily meeting for Developers.
·       Helps check progress toward the Sprint Goal.
·       Team members identify obstacles and coordinate work.
·       It is usually limited to 15 minutes.
3. Sprint Review
·       Takes place at the end of a sprint.
·       The team presents the completed work to stakeholders.
·       Feedback is collected about the product.
·       The Product Backlog may be updated based on feedback.
4. Sprint Retrospective
·       Takes place after the Sprint Review and before the next Sprint Planning.
·       The team discusses what went well.
·       The team identifies problems and areas for improvement.
·       It agrees on ways to improve quality and teamwork.


Scrum Terms and Their Purpose
1. Product Backlog
·       Contains the work needed to improve the product.
·       Includes features, bug fixes, improvements, and other requirements.
·       The Product Owner is accountable for effective backlog management.
·       Items are ordered according to value, priority, and other considerations.
2. Sprint Backlog
·       Contains the Sprint Goal, selected Product Backlog items, and the plan for delivering them.
·       Is created and updated by the Developers.
·       Helps the team track work during the sprint.
·       Can change as the team learns more, while maintaining focus on the Sprint Goal.
3. Sprint Goal
·       Describes the main objective of the sprint.
·       Gives the team a shared direction.
·       Helps the team make decisions during development.
·       Provides a clear purpose for the selected work.
4. Definition of Done (DoD)
·       Describes the quality requirements that work must meet to be considered complete.
·       Ensures the team follows consistent quality standards.
·       May include coding, testing, code review, and documentation requirements.
·       Helps prevent unfinished work from being treated as completed.
